5.1 Explaining heterogeneity

ToolQuestionRules
Subgroup analysisDoes the effect differ between categories (e.g. adults vs children)?Pre-specify (≤ 3–5), test the difference between subgroups (not "significant here, not there"), few studies per subgroup → low power
Meta-regressionDoes effect change with a continuous covariate (age, dose, year)?Rule of thumb ≥ 10 studies per covariate; observational by nature
Sensitivity analysisDoes the conclusion survive different choices?Pre-specified list; report all results
Reviewer trap
  • "Significant in men, not in women, therefore different." Wrong. Only a formal interaction (subgroup difference) test answers that.
  • Dredging 12 subgroups after seeing the data → false positives. Label post-hoc as exploratory.
  • Between-study comparisons are confounded; subgroup findings do not prove causation.

Standard sensitivity checks

  • Leave-one-out: drop each study in turn; does the estimate or I² collapse? (Trial C in Day 4's example.)
  • Exclude high-RoB studies.
  • Fixed vs random; DL vs REML; with/without HKSJ.
  • Exclude studies with imputed SDs or extreme sizes.
  • Alternative effect measure (RR vs OR vs RD).
High-yield

If the conclusion flips under reasonable alternatives, say so and downgrade certainty (inconsistency / risk of bias). A robust result survives; a fragile one is reported as fragile.

5.2 Publication bias and small-study effects

{{FUNNEL_SYM}}
Symmetric: no obvious asymmetry
{{FUNNEL_ASYM}}
Asymmetric: small "negative" studies missing?
  • Funnel plot: effect (x) vs precision/SE (y, inverted). Big precise studies at the top; small noisy ones spread at the bottom.
  • Egger's regression tests asymmetry. Use funnel/Egger/Begg only when there are ≥ 10 studies; fewer gives too little power to tell chance from asymmetry.
  • Trim-and-fill: estimates how many studies are "missing" and adjusts. Use as sensitivity, not truth.
  • Contour-enhanced funnels mark significance zones, helping to separate publication bias from other causes.
Mnemonic

HERO

Asymmetry has causes besides publication bias: Heterogeneity · Effect differs with study size (true) · Reporting bias · Other (chance, artefacts of the effect measure, poor methodological quality in small trials).

Reviewer trap

"Funnel looks symmetric, so no publication bias" with k = 6. You can't conclude that. Say the test is not interpretable; then rely on registry searches (are there registered but unpublished trials?) and grey literature.

5.3 GRADE: how much do you trust the pooled number?

GRADE rates certainty of evidence for each outcome — not each study. Start from the design, then move down for problems and up for strengths.

StartLevel
Randomised trialsHigh ⊕⊕⊕⊕
Observational (non-randomised)Low ⊕⊕○○ (ROBINS-I-based reviews can start high)

Downgrade (5)

  1. Risk of bias
  2. Inconsistency (heterogeneity unexplained)
  3. Indirectness (P/I/C/O differ from your question)
  4. Imprecision (wide CI, small total N / events)
  5. Publication bias

Each: not serious (0), serious (−1), very serious (−2).

Upgrade (3) — mainly observational

  1. Large effect (e.g. RR > 2 or < 0.5)
  2. Dose–response gradient
  3. All plausible confounding would reduce the effect (or suggest a spurious effect when none is seen)

Don't upgrade if you already downgraded for serious bias.

Mnemonic

RIIIP ↓   LDC ↑

Down: Risk of bias · Inconsistency · Indirectness · Imprecision · Publication bias. Up: Large effect · Dose-response · Confounding works against effect.

Final certaintyPlain-language meaning
HighWe are very confident the true effect is close to the estimate.
ModerateTrue effect is probably close, but may be substantially different.
LowOur confidence is limited; the true effect may be substantially different.
Very lowWe have very little confidence; the true effect is likely substantially different.

Summary of Findings (SoF) table: the page clinicians read

One row per important outcome: relative effect + CI, absolute effect per 1,000, no. of participants (studies), certainty rating with footnotes explaining each downgrade. Build it free in GRADEpro GDT.

Clinical pearl

Worked thinking, Day 4 example: RCTs start High. Some trials unblinded with subjective outcomes? −1 RoB. I² 45% with trials pointing opposite directions and a wide prediction interval? −1 inconsistency. CI crossing 1 plus possible harm? −1 imprecision. Final: Low, and you must say why.

Reviewer trap
  • Calling a "significant" p-value "high-quality evidence". Different questions.
  • Rating down without writing the reason in a footnote.
  • Applying GRADE to the whole review instead of outcome by outcome.

⚡ Day 5 · Rapid Revision

Explain it

  • Subgroup: test difference, pre-specify
  • Meta-regression: ≥10 studies
  • Sensitivity: leave-one-out, RoB, models

Bias

  • Funnel/Egger: ≥10 studies
  • Asymmetry ≠ always publication bias (HERO)
  • Trim-and-fill = sensitivity only

GRADE

  • 5 down: RIIIP
  • 3 up: LDC
  • RCT start High; observational start Low
  • Per outcome, with footnotes

Practice MCQs

1. Minimum number of studies usually advised for funnel plot / Egger's test:
A. 3 B. 5 C. 10 D. 30
Answer C. Below ~10 the tests have too little power.
2. Number of reasons to downgrade / upgrade in GRADE:
A. 5 / 3 B. 3 / 5 C. 4 / 4 D. 6 / 2
Answer A.
3. Starting certainty for RCTs in GRADE:
A. Low B. Moderate C. High D. Very low
Answer C.

Answer B.

7. Which can justify upgrading observational evidence?
