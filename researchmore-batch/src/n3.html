Day 3 · Evidence

Judge the evidence.

A trial being published doesn't make it trustworthy. Today you extract data cleanly and decide, domain by domain, how likely each result is to be wrong.

3.1 Data extraction

  1. Build the form before extracting: study ID, design, setting, population, intervention details, comparator, outcome definitions, time points, events/N per arm or mean/SD/N, funding, conflicts.
  2. Pilot on 2–3 studies. Fix the form. Then extract.
  3. Two reviewers independently (or one extracts, one verifies all). Compare. Resolve.
  4. Keep a source note: table/page number for every number.
Reviewer trap
  • Multi-arm trials: don't count the control group twice. Combine arms or split the shared group's N.
  • Multiple reports of one trial → one study. Link them. Counting a trial twice inflates precision.
  • Intention-to-treat vs per-protocol: take ITT where reported, and state which.
  • Medians/IQR to mean/SD: estimates exist but are approximations. Flag them and run a sensitivity analysis.
  • Missing data: contact the author (two attempts). Do not impute without a documented method.
Clinical pearl

Extract the same outcome at the same time point. "Pain at discharge" and "pain at 6 weeks" are different outcomes. Pre-specify which you will pool.

3.2 Risk of bias vs. quality vs. reporting

ConceptQuestion it asks
Risk of biasCould the result be systematically wrong because of how the study was designed or run?
Quality of reportingDid the authors describe what they did? (CONSORT)
PrecisionHow wide is the confidence interval? (a random-error issue, not bias)
High-yield

Bias = systematic error. Imprecision = random error. A small, perfectly run RCT is unbiased but imprecise. A huge, badly run one is precise but biased.

3.3 RoB 2: randomised trials

RoB 2 is the current Cochrane tool. It judges a specific result (one outcome from one trial), not the trial as a whole, and sorts each of its 5 domains into Low risk · Some concerns · High risk through signalling questions.

Mnemonic

R · D · M · M · S

Randomisation · Deviations · Missing data · Measurement · Selective reporting. Five domains; overall = worst domain (any High → High; otherwise any "Some concerns" → Some concerns).

Reviewer trap
  • Giving each study a single overall score from an old checklist and adding up points. Domain-based judgement is the standard.
  • Two reviewers must rate independently; report agreement and how disagreements were resolved.
  • Don't exclude trials just because they're high risk. Assess their influence in sensitivity analysis (Day 5).
  • Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (stars) is still seen in the literature for cohort/case-control; ROBINS-I is the Cochrane-favoured alternative; JBI tools exist for other designs. Name the tool in the protocol.
Hands-on · Day 3 deliverable
  1. Design your extraction form (≤ 25 columns). Pilot it on two papers.
  2. Apply RoB 2 (or ROBINS-I) to one paper's primary outcome; write one sentence of support per domain.
  3. Swap with a classmate: where did you disagree, and why?
  4. Write the protocol's Data extraction and Risk of bias sections.

Practice MCQs

1. RoB 2 contains how many domains?
A. 4 B. 5 C. 6 D. 7
Answer B. Randomisation, deviations, missing data, measurement, selection of reported result.
2. ROBINS-I contains how many domains?
A. 5 B. 6 C. 7 D. 9
Answer C. It adds confounding, participant selection, intervention classification.
3. A trial reports pain outcome with unblinded assessors in an open-label design. Which domain is most affected?
A. D1 B. D3 C. D4 D. D5
Answer C. Measurement of the outcome (subjective, unblinded).
4. The primary outcome in the paper differs from the registry entry. Domain:
A. D1 B. D2 C. D4 D. D5
Answer D. Selection of the reported result.
5. Overall RoB 2 judgement when one domain is High and the rest Low:
A. Low B. Some concerns C. High D. Average
Answer C. Worst-domain rule.
6. Which is a random-error issue rather than bias?
A. Poor allocation concealment B. Wide CI from small sample C. Selective outcome reporting D. Unblinded assessment
Answer B. Imprecision, handled in GRADE, not RoB.
7. Three-arm trial (A, B, control). To include in two separate pairwise MAs without double counting, you should:
A. Use full control in both B. Split the control group's N C. Drop control D. Ignore it
Answer B. Or combine arms if conceptually the same.
8. ROBINS-I's reference standard is:
A. A perfect RCT of the same question B. A cohort C. A case series D. Expert opinion
Answer A. The "target trial" you'd ideally have run.
