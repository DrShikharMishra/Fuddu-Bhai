6.1 The core idea

Definition

Direct evidence = trials that compare A with B head to head. Indirect evidence = A vs B inferred through a common comparator: d(A,B) = d(A,Pl) − d(B,Pl). Mixed (network) estimate = weighted combination of both.

  • Network gains precision and allows comparisons nobody trialled.
  • It adds assumptions. That's where reviewers attack.
High-yield

Pairwise first, network last. If your pairwise MAs are messy, the network will be messier. Build the geometry from the same included studies you've already extracted.

6.2 Geometry and vocabulary

TermMeaning

Connected networkEvery node is reachable. Disconnected networks cannot be analysed together

Reviewer trap
  • Node definition ("lumping vs splitting"): is "statin" one node or six doses/types? Define in the protocol, before looking at results.
  • Including a treatment that is never used in the clinical decision you care about. Network must be a decision set.
  • Showing the plot with equal-thickness lines. Size nodes by participants, edges by number of trials.

6.3 Assumption 1: Transitivity

Definition

Transitivity: studies comparing A–Pl and B–Pl are similar enough in effect modifiers (age, severity, baseline risk, dose, duration, setting) that an indirect A-vs-B comparison is valid. It's a clinical judgement about study characteristics; you assess it before looking at statistics.

  • Tabulate effect-modifier distributions per comparison (a "transitivity table").
  • If A–Pl trials are in severe disease and B–Pl trials in mild disease, the indirect A vs B is confounded by severity.
  • Nothing substitutes for judgement. Statistical tests can't prove it.

6.4 Assumption 2: Consistency (coherence)

Where direct and indirect evidence both exist, they should agree. Disagreement = inconsistency / incoherence.

LevelMethodQuestion
GlobalDesign-by-treatment interaction testAnywhere in the network, is there inconsistency?
LocalNode-splitting (SIDE) / loop inconsistencyFor this comparison, direct vs indirect: do they differ?
Mnemonic

T before C

Transitivity is the assumption (judge by thinking). Consistency is the testable consequence (check by statistics). If transitivity fails, consistency tests may or may not catch it.

Reviewer trap

"Test for inconsistency not significant → no inconsistency." Same lesson as I²: tests are underpowered. Present node-split estimates, not just p-values, and take clinical context into account.

6.5 Heterogeneity in a network

Most NMA models estimate a common τ² for all comparisons (an assumption). Heterogeneity within each pairwise comparison should still be examined (Day 4 skills). Network meta-regression can adjust for an effect modifier; needs enough trials.

6.6 Frequentist vs Bayesian

Both are acceptable. What matters is that the model, priors, convergence diagnostics (Bayesian) and software are reported. For beginners: MetaInsight (a free browser app built on R) runs frequentist and Bayesian NMA without coding and is ideal for teaching.

6.7 League tables and rankings

OutputWhat it shows
League tableEvery pairwise effect (with CI/CrI) in one matrix. The primary result.
Forest plot vs referenceAll treatments vs placebo
SUCRA / P-score0–100%: average rank, higher = more likely better. Not an effect size.
RankogramProbability of each treatment being 1st, 2nd, …
High-yield

Rankings must always appear next to the estimates and their certainty. A treatment can rank first on a trivial effect with a CI covering benefit and harm. SUCRA of 90% in a sparse, imprecise network is noise with a nice number on it.

Reviewer trap
  • "Treatment X is the best" written from SUCRA alone.
  • Ranking drugs that have few trials with other drugs that have many.
  • No statement of the clinical importance threshold. "Different" is not "better".

6.9 CINeMA: confidence in network estimates

CINeMA (Confidence in Network Meta-Analysis) is a free web app that rates each network estimate using 6 domains, then gives High / Moderate / Low / Very low.

#DomainIdea
1Within-study biasRoB of studies that contribute to the estimate (weighted by contribution)
2Reporting biasMissing or selectively reported results
3IndirectnessStudies' P/I/C/O vs your target question
4ImprecisionCI vs a clinically meaningful range
5HeterogeneityPrediction interval vs clinical thresholds
6IncoherenceDirect vs indirect disagreement (node-split), plus overall consistency
Mnemonic

W·R·I·I·H·I

Within-study bias · Reporting bias · Indirectness · Imprecision · Heterogeneity · Incoherence. Six boxes: tick each for no concerns / some / major.

Alternative: GRADE extended to NMA (Puhan et al. four-step approach). Cite whichever you use; CINeMA is the most practical for beginners because it is stepwise and software-supported.

Hands-on · Day 6 deliverable
  1. Sketch your network on paper: nodes, edges, and where direct evidence is missing.
  2. Build a transitivity table: 3 likely effect modifiers × each comparison.
  3. Explore MetaInsight with its demo data: find the league table, the ranking and the node-split result.
  4. Draft the NMA section of your protocol: node definition, models, consistency checks, ranking presentation, certainty method.
