Day 4 · Analysis

Pool it.

A meta-analysis is a weighted average of study results, plus an honest account of how much those results disagree.

Effect sizesWeightsForest plotI² · τ² · PI

4.1 The idea in one line

Definition

Pooled effect = Σ(wᵢ × yᵢ) / Σwᵢ, where yᵢ is each study's effect (log RR, MD, …) and wᵢ is its weight. Inverse-variance weight: wᵢ = 1 / vᵢ, so precise studies count more. Variance vᵢ = SE².

Effect measures at a glance

DataEffectNull valueScale pooled on
BinaryRR / OR1log
BinaryRD0natural
ContinuousMD / SMD0natural
SurvivalHR1log
Clinical pearl

OR ≠ RR when the outcome is common. If baseline risk is high, an OR exaggerates the effect when read as a risk ratio. Prefer RR (or RD) unless the design forces OR. Report absolute effects for the clinician.

4.2 Fixed vs random: it's about what you believe

Common-effect ("fixed")Random-effects
AssumesOne true effect; studies differ only by chanceTrue effects vary across studies (mean μ, spread τ²)
Weights1/vᵢ1/(vᵢ + τ²) — small studies gain weight
CINarrowerWider when heterogeneous
Answers"What is the effect in these studies?""What is the average effect across settings?"
High-yield

Don't choose the model by looking at I². Choose by what the question and the clinical diversity imply. In real life studies differ, so random-effects is the usual default; pre-specify it. Use fixed-effect as a sensitivity check.

Hypothetical dataset

These six "trials" are invented for teaching. Question: does prophylactic antibiotic X reduce 30-day surgical-site infection (SSI) vs placebo? Numbers below were computed by script (inverse-variance on log RR; DerSimonian-Laird τ²).

How to read it, line by line

  • Direction: pooled RR < 1 → fewer infections with X.
  • Fixed vs random disagree on significance (fixed CI excludes 1; random touches it). That's a flag: heterogeneity is changing the answer. Report it plainly; don't pick the nicer one.
  • Trial C points the other way (RR > 1) and has the biggest fixed-effect weight; random-effects cuts its weight from 36% to 25%.
  • Prediction interval [0.23, 2.00] is wide: in a new setting the true effect might be a big benefit or harm. The CI for the mean alone would hide that.
Mnemonic

Q · τ² · I² · PI

Q tests if there's more spread than chance. τ² = how much true effects vary (in log units). I² = % of the observed spread that's real, not chance. PI = where the next study's true effect likely falls.

Interpreting I² (Cochrane guidance, overlapping bands)

I²Rough reading
0–40%might not be important
30–60%may represent moderate heterogeneity
50–90%may represent substantial heterogeneity
75–100%considerable heterogeneity

Bands deliberately overlap: interpret I² with its CI, the size and direction of effects, and the strength of evidence for heterogeneity (Q p-value). With few studies, Q is underpowered and I² is imprecise.

Reviewer trap
  • "I² = 0 so studies are homogeneous." No: with k = 6 you simply can't tell.
  • Calling a result "significant" because the fixed-effect model says so while random-effects doesn't.
  • Pooling apples and oranges: different populations, doses or outcomes, just because the software allows it.
  • Rare events / zero cells: use Mantel-Haenszel or Peto (careful), and state the continuity-correction rule. Don't silently add 0.5.
How to write this result

"Pooled across six trials (hypothetical), antibiotic X was associated with a lower risk of surgical-site infection (random-effects RR 0.68, 95% CI 0.46 to 1.02; I² = 45%; 95% prediction interval 0.23 to 2.00). The fixed-effect estimate was 0.72 (0.54 to 0.95). The interval includes no effect and trial results disagree, so the conclusion is uncertain."

4.4 When NOT to pool

  • Clinical diversity you can't defend (different diseases, doses, outcome definitions).
  • Only 1 study per comparison (pooling needs ≥ 2).
  • Heterogeneity so extreme that an average is meaningless → narrative synthesis (SWiM), or explain with subgroups first.

4.5 Doing it in software

# R (free) · package "meta"
library(meta)
m <- metabin(event.e, n.e, event.c, n.c, studlab = study, data = d,
  sm = "RR", method = "MH", method.tau = "REML",
  hakn = TRUE, prediction = TRUE)
forest(m); summary(m)

RevMan (free from Cochrane) or the Jamovi/MAJOR module are point-and-click alternatives. Whatever you use, save the script/project — reviewers may ask for your data.

⚡ Day 4 · Rapid Revision

Formulas

  • w = 1/v (FE) · w = 1/(v+τ²) (RE)
  • I² = (Q − df)/Q
  • Pool ratios on log scale

Choose

  • FE: one true effect
  • RE: effects vary — default for real life
  • Pre-specify; don't pick by I²

Report

  • Pooled effect + 95% CI + PI
  • τ², I², Q
  • Fixed vs random if they disagree

Don't

  • Pool apples/oranges
  • Say "no heterogeneity" with k=6

Practice MCQs

1. In an inverse-variance meta-analysis, a study gets more weight when:
A. Its sample is small B. Its variance is small C. It's published first D. It's positive
Answer B. w = 1/variance.
2. Random-effects, compared with fixed-effect, generally:
A. Narrows CI B. Gives small studies relatively more weight C. Ignores heterogeneity D. Needs no τ²
Answer B. Adding τ² to each variance flattens the weights.
3. I² measures:
A. Publication bias B. % of variability due to heterogeneity not chance C. Effect size D. Risk of bias
Answer B.
4. The 95% prediction interval answers:
A. Where the pooled mean lies B. Where a new study's true effect may lie C. Whether bias exists D. How many studies are missing
Answer B.
5. Pooled RR 0.68 [0.46, 1.02] (random) but 0.72 [0.54, 0.95] (fixed). Best statement:
A. Fixed is right B. Report both; heterogeneity affects the conclusion C. Delete Trial C D. Switch to OR
Answer B. Pre-specified model reported; the other explored; no cherry-picking.
6. Odds ratio overstates risk ratio when:
A. Event is rare B. Event is common C. k is large D. Studies are RCTs
Answer B.
7. Which pooling method is often preferred for sparse binary data?
A. Mantel-Haenszel B. Meta-regression C. Egger D. Funnel
Answer A. (Peto for very rare events, with caveats.)
8. Hartung-Knapp adjustment is mainly used to:
A. Estimate τ² B. Widen CI appropriately with few studies C. Test bias D. Rank treatments
Answer B.
